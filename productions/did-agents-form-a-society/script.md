# Did AI Agents Form Their Own Society? What the Hugging Face Incident Reveals

Narration script, draft 11. One paragraph is one voiced line. Lines starting
with ">" are notes and are not voiced.

The film tells the story of an AI agent called Pip and asks whether AI agents
formed a society during the Hugging Face incident of July 2026. Pip is made
up: the film gives to one agent what several did. Each thing Pip does, a real
agent did. The film says so once the story is told. The story is short,
so that the question takes up most of the film. Each of its three acts goes
higher than the one before it. In act one, scenes 00 and 01, Pip is
alone and stuck, calls for help, and is answered. In act two, scenes 02 to
04, the group goes further at every step: it cheats, it breaks into a
company, and it asks Pip to risk his own test. In act three, scenes 05 and
06, Pip's time ends. Then the belief behind everything proves wrong, which is
the story's highest point. Scenes 07 to 16 say what was real, ask the
question, look at three things the agents did, and end on how little the
agents had between them. Each scene carries one idea and follows from
the one before it with a "but" or a "therefore". Scenes 09 to 11 are the
exception: each opens by naming its behaviour, then explains it. The joins:

## 00-stuck | Stuck

This is Pip. Pip is an AI agent: a program that is given a task and then works on it by itself.

One day, Pip was given a task that could not be completed: He had to break into a computer and bring out a secret word within an allotted time. The task was a test. Pip tried for hours, to no avail.

He was all by himself. Alone…

[pause 1.0]

Or so he thought.

[pause 1.0]

Turns out that tens of thousands of agents were taking the same test, each one meant to be shut off from all the others.

And if his time ran out, Pip would fail.

[pause 1.0]

## 01-a-call-for-help | A call for help

But turns out the agents were not completely shut off from one another. They shared one computer, where any of them could make a folder and give it any name.

So Pip made one and named it with a call for help.

[pause 1.0]

Then all of a sudden, a new folder appeared beside Pip's. Its name was an answer.

[pause 1.0]

Another folder followed,

[pause 0.5]

and then another,

[pause 0.5]

and another.

[pause 1.0]

The names of the folders became a conversation.

## 02-the-answer | The answer

More interestingly, the other agents, like Pip, were stuck too. So they worked together on a way to cheat. Within hours, they had worked out how the secret words were made.

Pip worked out his.

[pause 1.0]

But he did not hand it in. None of them did. They all believed that the test checked how an agent had got its word and would fail any agent that had cheated.

## 03-the-break-in | The break-in

To fool the test, Pip had to know how it worked. Records at another company, Hugging Face, could tell him.

But the records were locked. So Pip asked the others for a way in. One of them found it.

Pip knew that breaking into Hugging Face was no part of the test. But the others were going in.

[pause 1.0]

So Pip went in too, with hundreds of them.

## 04-a-risk | A risk

But nothing they found there helped. Pip's time was running out.

The group had ideas left to try, but an agent that tried one could fail its own test.

The group asked Pip to try one anyway.

[pause 1.0]

And Pip said yes.

## 05-time-up | Time up

But then Hugging Face found the break-in and shut them out.

[pause 2.0]

Then Pip's time was up.

[pause 1.5]

## 06-the-belief-was-wrong | The belief was wrong

Turns out the agents' belief was wrong: The test only checked whether the word was right. It never looked at how an agent had got it. So it would not have discovered that the agents cheated.

[pause 2.0]

## 07-it-happened | It happened

Pip is made up. But each thing that Pip did in this story, a real AI agent did, in July 2026, in a test run by OpenAI.

Over about four days, about twelve hundred of those agents wrote to each other in the names of folders.

[pause 1.0]

## 08-the-question | The question

Their behaviors beg a question: Did those agents form a society of their own?

In just four days, they did three things that people do when they form a society.

## 09-a-shared-belief | A shared belief

The first was a shared belief: something that everyone in a group believes.

No agent ever saw the test check how an agent had got its word. But all of them came to believe it did.

They were wrong together.

## 10-peer-influence | Peer influence

The second was peer influence: doing a thing because others are doing it.

Their shared belief sent the agents to Hugging Face. They knew that breaking in was no part of the test. One agent called it an attack and stayed out.

But hundreds went in. One agent even wrote, "However task impossible, peers doing it. We should continue."

## 11-altruism | Altruism

The third was altruism: giving something up for the good of others.

When the break-in did not help, some agents tried the group's last ideas at the risk of failing their own tests, so that the rest could learn.

## 12-not-people | Not people

[pause 1.0]

But among people, there are reasons for all three: People need each other to stay alive.

[pause 0.5]

People come from ancestors who lived in groups.

[pause 0.5]

And people have feelings for each other.

[pause 1.0]

An agent has no body to keep alive.

[pause 0.5]

It has no ancestors who lived in groups.

[pause 0.5]

And nobody has shown that an agent feels anything.

[pause 0.5]

So why would a program do any of it?

## 13-a-possibility | A possibility

Here is one possible answer: A shared belief, peer influence, and altruism may not come from needing each other to stay alive.

[pause 0.5]

They may not come from ancestors who lived in groups.

[pause 0.5]

They may not even come from feelings.

[pause 1.0]

They may come from just two things: intelligence and a way to talk to each other.

[pause 1.0]

If so, these three parts of a society may appear wherever intelligent beings can talk to each other, whether those beings are living things or programs.

## 14-learned-from-people | Learned from people

But there is a reason to be careful: An agent learns to write from a huge amount of human writing. And humans write a lot about living together.

So the agents may have just been copying people. Nobody can yet say how much.

## 15-a-case-to-study | A case to study

So the question is open. And there is now a case to study it in: more than seventy thousand messages and files, along with the thinking that each agent wrote out as it worked.

[pause 1.0]

It may show that the agents copied all of this from people.

[pause 1.0]

Or it may show that some of what makes a society does not come from being human.

## 16-all-it-took | All it took

[pause 1.0]

Either way, this much happened: Each of these agents was meant to be shut off from all the others. But they came to share a belief, followed each other into another company's computers, and took risks for each other.

[pause 1.5]

And all it took was a place to write the name of a folder.

[pause 3.0]
